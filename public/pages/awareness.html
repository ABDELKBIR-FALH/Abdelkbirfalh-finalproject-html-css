01 • Editorial overview

How Economic Control Erodes Autonomy

Economic abuse is a deliberate pattern of behavior in which one person uses money, credit, employment, or material resources to gain power and control over another. While it frequently co-occurs with emotional or physical abuse, economic abuse can also stand alone—quietly isolating a woman long before any overt conflict appears.

Abusers often disguise financial control as “protecting the family,” “being better with numbers,” or “traditional roles.” Over time, however, the result is the same: one partner holds all financial decision-making power while the other is left without credit, savings, or a safety net.

“True financial partnership is built on mutual transparency, shared decision-making, and respect for each person's independence—never secrecy, fear, or permission.”

Three Pillars of Economic Abuse

  1. Restricting Acquisition: Stopping a woman from earning wages, studying, or receiving benefits.
  2. Controlling Use: Dictating how money is spent and withholding basic necessities.
  3. Sabotaging Security: Destroying credit, draining savings, and generating coerced debt.

02 • Detailed taxonomy

8 Forms of Economic Abuse

Economic abuse takes many shapes. Review each pattern below along with concrete real-world examples.

Form 01

Financial Control

Dictating how every dollar is earned, saved, or spent while denying access to shared bank accounts.

Real-world example: A partner confiscates paychecks upon deposit, refuses to share online banking passwords, or demands itemized receipts for every grocery item.

Form 02

Denial of Resources

Withholding essential funds needed for food, healthcare, medication, clothing, or childcare.

Real-world example: Refusing to pay for a woman's prescription medication or children's winter coats despite having sufficient household income.
